Gemini vs GPT for Document Analysis: Which Is Better?

Both models are capable. But for document analysis specifically, they have meaningful differences in context window size, instruction following, and structured output quality that matter for real workloads.

N
Norcanto Team
·December 24, 2024·9 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Gemini 1.5 Flash supports up to 1 million token context — far larger than GPT-4 Turbo
  • Both models produce comparable quality summaries on short documents under 10 pages
  • For very long documents, Gemini has a structural advantage due to its larger context window
  • Structured JSON output quality is comparable between the two with well-crafted prompts
  • Gemini is significantly cheaper per token than GPT-4, making it more practical for high-volume use

If you are building or evaluating an AI document analysis tool, the choice of underlying model matters. Both Google Gemini and OpenAI GPT-4 are capable of impressive document understanding, but they have real differences that affect performance, cost, and practical usability for document workloads.

We tested both on a range of real document types: a 45-page commercial lease, a 120-page corporate annual report, a 25-page research paper, and a standard 8-page SaaS vendor agreement. Here is what we found.

Context Window: The Most Important Difference

The context window is how much text a model can hold in "working memory" at once. This is the single most important factor for document analysis.

Gemini 1.5 Flash supports up to 1 million tokens — roughly 750,000 words or about 1,500 pages of dense text. GPT-4 Turbo supports 128,000 tokens, which is approximately 96,000 words or about 190 pages. GPT-4o has a similar limit.

For most individual documents, neither limit is a constraint. A 50-page contract is well within both. But for very long documents like full annual reports, comprehensive vendor due diligence packages, or multi-document analysis tasks, Gemini's larger context window is a meaningful practical advantage.

Summary Quality on Short Documents

On documents under 20 pages, both models produce excellent summaries. Quality differences come down to prompting. With well-crafted, specific prompts asking for structured output, both models reliably extract the most important information in a clear, organized format.

For plain-language explanations — converting legal or technical jargon into accessible language — both perform well. GPT-4 has a slight edge on stylistic fluency. Gemini tends to be slightly more literal in its interpretations.

Structured Output and JSON Reliability

For document analysis tools that need the AI to return structured data — a JSON object with specific fields for risks, dates, obligations, and actions — both models are reliable with appropriate prompting. Gemini tends to follow JSON structure instructions more consistently when given explicit field definitions. GPT-4 occasionally adds conversational framing around the JSON unless specifically instructed not to.

Practical takeaway: If your use case involves processing large volumes of long documents, Gemini's context window advantage and lower cost per token make it the more practical choice. For lower-volume work where stylistic output quality is paramount, GPT-4 is also excellent.

Cost Comparison

Gemini 1.5 Flash is highly efficient for large document workloads. For a document analysis tool processing thousands of documents per month, this efficiency difference is substantial.
