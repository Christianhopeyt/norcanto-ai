How to Use AI to Stay Compliant with Regulatory Documents

Regulatory requirements, industry standards, and internal compliance frameworks all live inside dense documents that most teams never read fully. AI extracts the obligations and action items so you can actually act on them.

N
Norcanto Team
·November 27, 2024·9 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Compliance failures often happen not because requirements are unknown but because they are buried in unread documents
  • AI extracts specific obligations and action items from regulatory documents, standards, and frameworks
  • Document chat lets you ask whether specific practices meet documented requirements
  • AI helps identify gaps between what your policies say and what regulations require
  • Compliance document libraries can be analyzed systematically to build a complete obligation register

Compliance failures are rarely the result of intentional wrongdoing. They happen because the requirements were buried in a 200-page regulatory document that no one read fully, or because the obligation was present in the document but never translated into a concrete action item with a responsible owner.

AI document analysis addresses both of these failure modes directly. Here is how to build a compliance workflow around it.

The Compliance Document Problem

Regulatory documents are some of the densest, most complex texts that exist. The GDPR is 88 pages of legal text. An ISO 27001 standard runs to over 30 pages of technical requirements. Industry-specific regulations like HIPAA, PCI DSS, or local financial services regulations can run to hundreds of pages of detailed requirements.

Even within organizations, internal compliance frameworks — information security policies, code of conduct documents, supplier codes — are often long enough that the people who need to follow them have never read them in full. The result is that compliance becomes a box-checking exercise rather than a genuine operational commitment.

Step 1: Build Your Obligation Register

The most important compliance artifact is an obligation register — a comprehensive list of every specific thing your organization must do, must not do, or must have in place to meet its regulatory and contractual requirements. Building this manually from regulatory documents is an enormous task. AI makes it practical.

Upload each regulatory document, standard, or framework that applies to your organization. For each one, the AI analysis extracts the specific obligations in the document. Collect these across all your compliance documents and you have the foundation of a comprehensive obligation register in a fraction of the time it would take manually.

Step 2: Check Your Current Policies Against Requirements

Once you know what is required, the next step is checking whether your current policies and procedures actually meet those requirements. This gap analysis is where AI document chat is particularly powerful.

Upload your privacy notice alongside the GDPR's Article 13 requirements and ask: "Does this privacy notice include all the information required by Article 13?" Upload your information security policy alongside ISO 27001 controls and ask: "Which of the Annex A controls are addressed in this policy and which appear to be missing?"

The AI responses give you a structured starting point for your gap analysis — not a definitive legal opinion, but a systematic first pass that identifies where to focus your attention.

Important note: AI gap analysis is a tool for identifying areas that need expert review, not a substitute for it. Regulatory compliance decisions should always be validated by qualified legal or compliance professionals for your specific jurisdiction and circumstances.

Step 3: Extract Action Items from Compliance Documents

Many regulatory documents contain explicit action requirements — things that must be done by specific dates, things that must be reported to authorities, things that must be documented. These are exactly the kind of structured obligations that AI extracts as action items.

For example, GDPR contains specific requirements about breach notification timelines (72 hours to the supervisory authority), data subject request response times (one month), and documentation requirements for processing activities. AI analysis of the GDPR extracts these specific, actionable requirements clearly — they become the action items for your compliance program rather than obligations buried in legal text.

Step 4: Monitor for Changes

Regulations change. Standards are updated. When a new version of a regulatory document is published, you need to understand what changed and what new obligations it creates. AI analysis of the new version compared against your notes from the previous version makes this change management process systematic and fast.

High-Value Compliance Use Cases

GDPR and data protection: Extracting all processing obligations, data subject rights requirements, and documentation requirements from the regulation and its guidance documents.

Financial services regulation: Analyzing regulatory circulars and guidance documents to extract specific compliance obligations for your license category.

Employment law compliance: Reviewing labor law updates to identify which changes require handbook revisions or policy updates.

Supplier and vendor compliance: Checking supplier contracts and codes of conduct against your own supplier compliance requirements to identify gaps.

Audit preparation: Using AI to systematically verify that your documented policies and procedures address each item on an audit checklist before the audit begins.
