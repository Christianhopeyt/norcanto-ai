Is AI Document Analysis Secure? What You Need to Know

Before uploading sensitive documents to any AI tool, you need to understand data handling, retention policies, API security, and what actually happens to your content once it leaves your device.

N
Norcanto Team
·November 30, 2024·8 min read
Key Takeaways
  • There is a significant difference between tools that store your documents and those that process-and-discard
  • API-based AI tools that use Google or OpenAI are governed by those companies' data handling policies
  • Understand whether your documents are used to train AI models — most enterprise APIs explicitly opt out
  • Transport encryption protects data in transit but tells you nothing about what happens to it on the server
  • For highly confidential documents, on-premise AI solutions may be more appropriate than cloud tools

AI document analysis requires sending the content of your documents to an AI service for processing. For most documents — vendor agreements, research papers, reports — this is a straightforward privacy tradeoff. For highly confidential documents — M&A targets, litigation strategy, employee personal data — it requires more careful thought.

Here is a complete framework for evaluating whether an AI document tool is safe to use with your specific documents.

The Core Question: What Happens to Your Document?

When you upload a document to an AI analysis tool, one of three things happens to the content:

  • Process and discard: The content is sent to an AI API, the analysis is returned, and the original content is not stored persistently on the tool's servers
  • Store for your use: The content is stored so you can access past analyses and ask follow-up questions
  • Store and potentially use for training: The content is stored and may be used to improve AI models

The first option is the most privacy-protective. The third is the most concerning for confidential documents. Understanding which applies to your chosen tool is the most important question to ask.

How Norcanto AI Handles Your Documents

Norcanto AI uses Google Gemini API to process documents. The document content is sent to Google's API for analysis. Gemini API, when used by third-party applications, does not use submitted data to train Google's models by default — this is governed by Google's API terms. The analysis results are what gets stored to enable your history and document chat, not the raw document content.

For highly sensitive documents, you should review Google's current API data handling terms, as these govern how your content is processed at the AI layer.

Transport Security

Legitimate AI document tools use HTTPS for all data in transit, meaning your document content is encrypted between your browser and the tool's servers, and between the tool's servers and the AI provider. This is table stakes for any reputable web service.

Transport encryption tells you the data is safe while moving. It says nothing about what happens once it arrives. A tool can use perfect transport encryption while storing your documents indefinitely on unsecured servers. Both questions matter.

AI Training Data Policies

One of the most important questions for business document use is whether your documents are used to train AI models. This matters because training data can theoretically surface in AI outputs for other users.

Major AI API providers including Google and OpenAI have explicit policies for their enterprise and API tiers that exclude customer data from model training by default. Consumer-facing tools that use AI may have different policies. Always check the terms of service for the specific tool you are using, not just the AI provider behind it.

Practical test: Before uploading a sensitive document, ask yourself: if this document's content somehow appeared in a news article or a competitor's hands, what would the consequences be? Let the answer guide how carefully you scrutinize the tool's security practices.

Document Categories and Risk Levels

Low sensitivity (safe for standard cloud AI tools): Publicly available reports, published research papers, standard vendor agreements for routine services, general company policies.

Medium sensitivity (review the tool's data policies first): Client contracts with confidential business information, internal financial reports not intended for public disclosure, employee handbook updates.

High sensitivity (consider on-premise solutions or consult your privacy team): M&A transaction documents, litigation materials, documents containing personal health or financial information, government classified materials.

Questions to Ask Any AI Document Tool

  • Is document content stored after analysis is complete?
  • Is submitted content used to train AI models?
  • Which AI provider processes the content and what are their data handling terms?
  • Is the service compliant with GDPR, HIPAA, or other relevant regulations?
  • What are the data deletion policies and how can users request deletion?
  • Is the service SOC 2 certified or does it have equivalent security certification?

Any reputable tool should be able to answer these questions clearly. Vague answers are a red flag.
