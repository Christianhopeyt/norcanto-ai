Using AI for Due Diligence: A Complete Checklist

Whether you are reviewing an acquisition target, a new vendor, or a partnership agreement, AI document analysis dramatically compresses the time it takes to do thorough due diligence.

N
Norcanto Team
·December 15, 2024·10 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Due diligence document review is one of the highest-value use cases for AI analysis
  • A standard vendor due diligence package can include 20 or more documents — AI processes all of them
  • AI surfaces inconsistencies across documents that human reviewers often miss under time pressure
  • Structured risk reports make it easy to prioritize which issues need professional follow-up
  • AI due diligence is a starting point, not a replacement for professional advisors on significant transactions

Due diligence is one of the most document-intensive processes in business. A standard vendor onboarding review might involve reviewing a service agreement, master terms, data processing agreement, security questionnaire, insurance certificates, and references. An M&A transaction can involve thousands of pages across dozens of categories.

AI document analysis does not replace the judgment of experienced advisors. But it dramatically accelerates the initial review phase and ensures nothing is missed before the experts engage.

The Due Diligence Document Categories

Depending on the type of due diligence, you may need to review documents across several categories. For vendor due diligence, the key categories are: contractual terms and obligations, data privacy and security practices, financial stability indicators, insurance and liability coverage, and regulatory compliance documentation. For acquisition due diligence, add: corporate governance documents, intellectual property ownership, employment agreements, litigation history, and financial statements.

AI document analysis works across all of these categories. The extraction of risks, obligations, key dates, and action items is equally valuable whether you are reviewing an NDA or a corporate charter.

The AI Due Diligence Workflow

Step 1: Compile your document package. Collect all documents in the due diligence package into a single folder. Request everything you need upfront rather than in rounds — it saves time and signals professionalism.

Step 2: Process each document through AI analysis. Upload each document to Norcanto AI and save the analysis report. For a 20-document vendor package, this takes roughly 20 minutes versus the 8 to 12 hours a traditional first-pass review would require.

Step 3: Consolidate the risks. Collect the risk flags from all document analyses into a single list, sorted by severity. This consolidated risk register becomes the working document for your due diligence review.

Step 4: Identify inconsistencies. Compare key terms across documents. Does the master service agreement's liability cap match what the order form references? Does the data processing agreement cover all the data types mentioned in the privacy policy? AI analysis of each document makes these cross-document comparisons much faster.

Step 5: Prioritize for professional review. Take your consolidated risk register to your legal, financial, or technical advisors. Focus their time on the specific issues the AI flagged as high severity. This is where you save the most money — advisors reviewing targeted issues rather than reading full documents from scratch.

The Due Diligence Checklist

For each document in your package, confirm that AI analysis has addressed the following:

  • Key obligations on both parties clearly identified
  • Liability provisions reviewed including caps, carve-outs, and indemnification
  • Termination rights and notice periods for both parties
  • Auto-renewal provisions and opt-out windows
  • IP ownership and assignment provisions
  • Data handling, privacy, and security obligations
  • Governing law and dispute resolution mechanisms
  • Change of control provisions (especially important for acquisitions)
  • Representations and warranties and their survival period
  • Material adverse change definitions

Red flag to watch for: Any document where the AI analysis returns an unusually large number of high-severity risks deserves immediate escalation to a professional advisor before proceeding further in the process.

What AI Cannot Do in Due Diligence

AI document analysis cannot verify that representations are accurate. It cannot assess whether disclosed litigation is likely to result in adverse outcomes. It cannot evaluate the reasonableness of financial projections or the quality of a management team. These judgments require human expertise, industry knowledge, and often external verification.

Think of AI as doing the reading and the first-pass flagging. The experienced advisors do the interpreting and the decision-making. This division of labor is where the efficiency gains are largest.
